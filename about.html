Our story

From a shared spreadsheet to 12,400 doctors

In 2019, four emergency medicine registrars preparing for MRCEM started keeping a shared spreadsheet of the questions they kept getting wrong, and why. It grew to 900 rows before anyone suggested it might be useful to other people.

The problem they had run into is one every emergency medicine candidate recognises. General medical question banks treat emergency medicine as a thin slice between cardiology and surgery. Dedicated EM resources existed, but most were written years earlier, rarely updated against changing guidelines, and almost never mapped to the actual published blueprint. Candidates were working extremely hard against material that could not tell them whether they were ready.

EM Hub launched properly in 2021 with a single MRCEM SBA course and 1,200 questions. The founding principle then is the one we still hold to now: a question earns its place in the bank only if it maps to a specific curriculum item and has been reviewed by a second practising consultant. Nothing is included because it was easy to write.

Five years on the platform carries seven programmes, 12,800 questions and sixteen faculty across the UK, India and the Gulf. More than 12,400 doctors have prepared with it, and 94% of the 2025 cohort passed at their first attempt. The spreadsheet is still in the archive.

What we deliberately do not do

  • We do not outsource question writing. Every item is authored by a named faculty member who works clinically.
  • We do not sell rolling subscriptions. You buy a fixed duration. Nothing auto-renews, and nothing needs cancelling.
  • We do not pad the bank. Roughly 8% of questions are retired or rewritten each exam cycle rather than left to age.
  • We do not guarantee a pass. Nobody honestly can. We guarantee an accurate picture of where you stand.

Mission

To make high-quality emergency medicine exam preparation available to every doctor sitting these examinations, regardless of where they trained or where they work.

Concretely, that means three commitments. Content that is accurate on the day you read it. Analytics honest enough to tell you when you are not ready. And pricing that a trainee on a single salary in any country can justify.

Vision

An emergency medicine workforce where passing the examination and being ready for the resus room are the same thing.

Exams are a proxy for competence, and a good preparation platform should narrow the gap between the two rather than widen it. Everything we build is judged against that: does this make a better emergency physician, or only a better test-taker?
